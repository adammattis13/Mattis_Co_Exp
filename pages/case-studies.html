Transformation & Change Global Education Consortium

Enabling a $3B+ Divestiture Through Operational Unification

Situation

A global education consortium of 86 universities across 200+ campuses and five continents faced an existential challenge. The higher education market was contracting, and the organization needed to transform its operations to remain competitive and facilitate a strategic divestiture. But the consortium operated as a loose federation—each university running independently, duplicating work, and struggling to deliver a consistent experience to learners.

The traditional model of developing courses and degree programs on an annual cadence within each university proved too slow to keep pace with market demand. The same degrees and courses were being created multiple times across the network. Leadership recognized that without dramatic change, they would lose learners to more agile institutions.

Approach

We led the design and implementation of a unified operating model that fundamentally changed how the consortium worked. The transformation centered on three pillars:

Operational Unification: We streamlined common functions across all 86 universities, eliminating redundancy while preserving local market responsiveness. This included centralizing curriculum development, creating shared infrastructure, and establishing enterprise-wide governance.

Technology Modernization: We migrated data centers to the cloud and redesigned technology operations to support the new operating model. This wasn't a lift-and-shift—it was a fundamental rearchitecting of how technology enabled the learner experience.

Experience Design: We redesigned operations around the optimal learner experience, from enrollment to graduation. Every process was evaluated against a simple question: does this serve the learner?

Outcome

Time-to-market for new degree programs dropped by 75%—from over a year to a single quarter. The organization achieved operational consistency across five continents while maintaining local market relevance. The transformation significantly reduced the debt profile and positioned the consortium for a successful divestiture valued at over $3 billion.

Perhaps more importantly, the new operating model created a sustainable competitive advantage. The consortium could now respond to market demand for new skills faster than traditional universities, delivering relevant programs when learners needed them.

Operating Model Design A Large Shipping Provider

Unifying Nine Global Operating Companies

Situation

A large global shipping company had grown through acquisition, resulting in nine independent operating companies spread across multiple continents. Each company had its own systems, processes, and culture. Packages moving through the network crossed organizational boundaries multiple times, creating handoff delays, tracking inconsistencies, and an unpredictable customer experience.

Leadership knew integration was necessary, but previous attempts had stalled. The operating companies had strong regional identities and legitimate concerns about losing local responsiveness to a centralized model.

Approach

We developed an integration strategy that balanced global consistency with regional autonomy. Rather than forcing a single operating model onto all nine companies, we identified the critical integration points that would deliver customer value and focused there.

The strategy addressed four domains: tracking visibility across the entire network, standardized package handling at handoff points, unified technology operations, and an optimized cost structure. Each domain had clear outcomes, defined governance, and a phased implementation plan.

We designed the organizational structure to support the new model—defining roles, responsibilities, and decision rights across the integrated entity while preserving the operational capabilities that made each regional company successful.

Outcome

The integration created end-to-end package visibility for the first time in the company's history. Customers could track shipments seamlessly across all nine operating companies. Handoff delays decreased significantly, and the predictability of delivery windows improved.

The unified technology operations reduced costs while improving reliability. And the new operating model provided a foundation for future acquisitions—a repeatable playbook for integrating new companies into the network.

Operating Model Design A Non-Profit Hospital System

Reclaiming IT Operations: $40M in Annual Savings

Situation

A non-profit hospital system had outsourced its IT operations to a large external vendor years earlier. What was intended to reduce costs and improve service had achieved neither. The vendor relationship had become adversarial, costs had escalated, and the hospital system had lost the internal capability to manage technology strategically.

Worse, the outsourcing arrangement had created distance between IT and the clinical mission. Technology decisions were being made based on vendor economics rather than patient outcomes. Leadership wanted to reclaim control—but the organization had lost the muscle memory to run IT internally.

Approach

We designed a comprehensive insourcing strategy that went beyond simply bringing IT back in-house. The goal was to build an IT organization that could operate as a strategic partner to clinical leadership—not just a service provider.

The transformation included organizational design for the new IT function, capability building for staff transitioning from the vendor, governance structures that connected IT decisions to clinical outcomes, and a phased transition plan that maintained service continuity throughout the change.

We placed particular emphasis on the cultural shift required. The new IT organization needed to operate with a patient-first mindset, making technology decisions through the lens of clinical impact rather than technical elegance.

Outcome

The insourcing delivered $40 million in annual savings—far exceeding the original outsourcing promise. But the financial impact was secondary to the strategic benefits.

The hospital system regained control over its technology destiny. IT became a partner in clinical innovation rather than a cost center to be managed. Speed of execution improved dramatically as the organization could move without negotiating change orders with an external vendor. And the patient focus returned to the center of technology decisions.

Digital & AI Strategy A Legacy Publishing Company

Repositioning a Publishing Icon for Digital-First

Situation

A world-renowned publishing house with a portfolio of iconic brands faced the digital reckoning that had disrupted the entire industry. Revenue from print was declining, digital revenue wasn't growing fast enough to compensate, and the organization was structured around a business model that no longer matched market reality.

The company had multiple brands operating independently, each with its own print operations, digital platforms, and overhead. Redundancy was everywhere. But consolidation attempts had failed because they threatened the editorial independence that made each brand valuable.

Approach

We helped leadership develop a digital-first strategy that preserved what made the brands valuable while eliminating the structural inefficiencies that were dragging down performance.

The strategy separated editorial independence from operational independence. Brands could maintain their distinct voices and editorial perspectives while sharing infrastructure, technology platforms, and back-office functions. We designed a new operating model that consolidated print operations, unified digital platforms, and eliminated redundancy across brands—without touching the editorial structures that defined each brand's identity.

We also helped the organization build new capabilities for the digital era: data-driven audience development, digital subscription models, and content strategies optimized for digital consumption rather than print adaptation.

Outcome

The consolidation of print operations and elimination of cross-brand redundancy delivered significant cost savings. More importantly, the digital-first strategy positioned the company for sustainable growth. Digital revenue accelerated as the organization stopped treating digital as a secondary channel and started building for it natively.

The brands maintained their editorial distinctiveness—readers couldn't tell that anything had changed. But behind the scenes, the company was operating with a fundamentally different cost structure and growth trajectory.

Operating Model Design A Large Financial & Insurance Institution

Redesigning HR Around the Employee Experience

Situation

A large financial and insurance institution recognized that its HR operations were designed around administrative efficiency rather than employee experience. The processes employees encountered—from hiring through retirement—were fragmented, inconsistent, and often frustrating. In a competitive talent market, the employee experience had become a liability.

HR technology was equally fragmented: multiple systems that didn't communicate, manual handoffs between processes, and no single view of the employee journey. The organization knew it needed to transform but wasn't sure where to start.

Approach

We guided the organization through a comprehensive redesign of HR operations and technology, centered on the employee experience. The work began with journey mapping—understanding every touchpoint an employee encounters from the moment they apply for a job through their eventual departure or retirement.

From this foundation, we designed an optimized and curated employee experience that eliminated friction, created consistency, and delivered moments that mattered. We then worked backward to define the operating model and technology architecture required to deliver that experience.

The transformation included process redesign, organizational restructuring of the HR function, technology selection and implementation, and change management to ensure adoption across the enterprise.

Outcome

The new HR operating model delivered a fundamentally different employee experience. Processes that once took weeks were completed in days. Employees had a single point of access for all HR needs. And the moments that mattered—onboarding, promotion, life events—were designed to reinforce the organization's culture and values.

Employee satisfaction with HR services improved dramatically. More importantly, the organization's ability to attract and retain talent improved as the employee experience became a competitive advantage rather than a liability.
