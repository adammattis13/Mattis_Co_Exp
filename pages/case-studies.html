Scaling · Exit Strategy Global Education Network

Scaling and Unifying 86 Institutions to Enable a $3B+ Exit

Situation

A global education network of 86 institutions across 200+ campuses and five continents had grown through acquisition but never unified its operations. Each institution ran independently—duplicating work, moving slowly, and struggling to deliver a consistent experience. The market was contracting, and leadership needed to transform the business to position for a major exit.

The traditional model was too slow to compete. The same programs were being created multiple times across the network. Without dramatic operational change, the business couldn't command the valuation leadership knew it was worth.

Approach

We designed and implemented a unified operating model that streamlined common functions across all 86 institutions while preserving local market responsiveness. The work centered on three priorities:

Operational Unification: Centralized curriculum development, created shared infrastructure, and established enterprise-wide governance—eliminating redundancy without killing what made each institution locally effective.

Infrastructure Modernization: Migrated data centers to the cloud and rearchitected technology operations to support the new model at scale.

Exit Positioning: Every operational improvement was designed with the end state in mind—building the consistency, cost structure, and growth trajectory that buyers look for.

Outcome

Time-to-market for new programs dropped by 75%. The organization achieved operational consistency across five continents while maintaining local relevance. The debt profile improved significantly, and the business successfully positioned for a divestiture valued at over $3 billion.

The unified operating model became a sustainable competitive advantage—enabling the network to respond to market demand faster than traditional competitors.

Scaling · M&A A Large Shipping Provider

Unifying Nine Operating Companies After a Decade of Acquisitions

Situation

A large global shipping company had grown through acquisition, resulting in nine independent operating companies across multiple continents. Each had its own systems, processes, and culture. Packages crossing the network hit organizational boundaries that created delays, tracking gaps, and an inconsistent customer experience.

Previous integration attempts had stalled. Regional companies had strong identities and legitimate concerns about losing local responsiveness to a centralized model.

Approach

Rather than forcing a single operating model onto all nine companies, we identified the critical integration points that would deliver customer value and focused there. The strategy addressed tracking visibility, standardized handoff processes, unified technology operations, and cost structure optimization.

We designed the organizational structure to support the integrated entity—defining roles, responsibilities, and decision rights while preserving the operational capabilities that made each regional company successful. This became the repeatable playbook for integrating future acquisitions.

Outcome

End-to-end package visibility was achieved for the first time in the company's history. Customers could track shipments seamlessly across all nine operating companies. Delivery predictability improved, technology costs decreased, and the new operating model provided a repeatable framework for integrating future acquisitions.

Advisory · Scaling A Non-Profit Hospital System

Reclaiming IT Operations: $40M in Annual Savings

Situation

A non-profit hospital system had outsourced its IT operations years earlier. What was intended to reduce costs had achieved the opposite—the vendor relationship became adversarial, costs escalated, and the organization lost the internal capability to manage technology strategically. Technology decisions were driven by vendor economics, not patient outcomes.

Approach

We designed a comprehensive insourcing strategy that went beyond simply bringing IT back in-house. The goal was to build an IT organization that operated as a strategic partner to clinical leadership. The work included organizational design, capability building for transitioning staff, governance structures tying IT decisions to clinical outcomes, and a phased transition plan that maintained service continuity.

Outcome

The insourcing delivered $40 million in annual savings—far exceeding the original outsourcing promise. The hospital regained control over its technology strategy. Speed of execution improved dramatically. And the patient focus returned to the center of technology decisions, making IT a competitive advantage rather than a cost center.

Advisory · Scaling A Legacy Publishing Company

Repositioning a Publishing Icon for Digital-First Growth

Situation

A world-renowned publishing house with a portfolio of iconic brands faced declining print revenue and digital growth that couldn't compensate. Multiple brands operated independently, each with its own operations, platforms, and overhead. Consolidation attempts had failed because they threatened the editorial independence that made each brand valuable.

Approach

We separated editorial independence from operational independence. Brands maintained their distinct voices while sharing infrastructure, technology, and back-office functions. The strategy consolidated print operations, unified digital platforms, and built new capabilities for digital-first growth—data-driven audience development, subscription models, and content strategies built for digital consumption.

Outcome

The consolidation delivered significant cost savings. Digital revenue accelerated as the organization stopped treating digital as a secondary channel. Brands maintained their editorial distinctiveness—readers couldn't tell anything had changed. But behind the scenes, the company operated with a fundamentally different cost structure and growth trajectory.

Scaling A Large Financial & Insurance Institution

Redesigning HR to Scale the Employee Experience

Situation

A large financial institution's HR operations were designed around administrative efficiency rather than employee experience. Processes were fragmented, technology systems didn't communicate, and in a competitive talent market, the employee experience had become a liability rather than an advantage.

Approach

We redesigned HR operations from the employee's perspective—mapping every touchpoint from application through departure. From that foundation, we designed an optimized experience that eliminated friction and created consistency. The work included process redesign, organizational restructuring, technology selection, and change management across the enterprise.

Outcome

Processes that took weeks were completed in days. Employees had a single point of access for all HR needs. Employee satisfaction improved dramatically, and the organization's ability to attract and retain talent turned from a liability into a competitive advantage.
