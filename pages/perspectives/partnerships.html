The Power of Partnerships

From BMX tracks to boardrooms, from combat zones to corporate strategy sessions—every stage of my life has been shaped by partnerships. Here's what three decades taught me.

Adam Mattis December 2024 8 min read

For me, partnerships aren't just a business strategy—they're a way of life. Over three decades, from racing BMX bikes as a teenager to overseeing strategic partnerships today, I've learned that success is rarely a solo endeavor.

The most meaningful wins, whether on a race track or in a boardroom, happen when you're helping others succeed alongside you.

The First Lesson

I was never fast enough to earn a BMX sponsorship the traditional way. But I needed a way to fund my racing habit, so I approached a local bicycle shop with a simple proposition: give me a jersey and a discount on parts, and I'll promote your shop far beyond the track.

That handshake deal taught me my first lesson about partnerships: it's not about what you can get—it's about what value you can create. Over the years, I brought them referrals and visibility, and in return, they invested more in supporting my racing. Both sides won.

Life and Death Partnerships

After a liquidity event from an early startup, I thought I was done with partnerships when I joined the Army. For the first year, focused on training and learning, that seemed true. But in my second year, when my unit began working with the Iraqi Police, those partnership lessons came roaring back.

Partnership takes on an entirely different meaning in life-and-death scenarios. Trust wasn't about business success—it was about survival. These experiences taught me that the strongest partnerships are forged when stakes are highest and mutual dependence is absolute.

The best partnerships happen when partners believe in your vision enough to invest in it—and you believe in theirs enough to do the same.

Learning the Other Side

After being wounded in combat and retiring from the Army, I returned to technology and spent several years learning management, advanced engineering, and architecture. Crucially, I also learned how to be a customer of vendors.

This flipped perspective taught me invaluable lessons about the other side of the partnership equation: what it's like when you're the one being partnered with, not just the one seeking partnership. The best partners understand both sides of the table.

Impact Over Dollars

My role running global business development at a major brand expanded my partnership responsibilities beyond just revenue. I managed partnerships with organizations like Wounded Warrior Project, Special Operations Warrior Foundation, and other mission-driven organizations.

This period taught me that the most powerful partnerships aren't always measured in revenue—they're measured in impact. Sometimes raising awareness matters more than dollars. The best partnerships serve a purpose bigger than profit.

Complex Ecosystems

During my time as an executive at a global consulting firm, I oversaw complex partnerships between the firm, vendors, and clients. These weren't simple bilateral relationships but intricate ecosystems where multiple parties had to find mutual value.

Here I learned about identifying mutual interests across complex stakeholder groups and balancing competing needs while maintaining profitability for everyone involved. The most sophisticated partnerships require orchestration, not just negotiation.

The Throughline

Whether you're a teenager trying to fund your racing dreams or an executive building strategic alliances, the fundamentals remain the same:

Create genuine value for others. Partnerships that start with "what can I get?" fail. Partnerships that start with "what can I contribute?" thrive.

Build relationships rooted in trust. Trust compounds over time. Every kept commitment, every honest conversation, every moment of reliability adds to the foundation.

Remember that the best partnerships make everyone better. If a partnership isn't creating mutual value, it isn't really a partnership—it's extraction with extra steps.

For me, this was a way of life long before it became a business philosophy. The context changes, but the truth remains: we're stronger together.
